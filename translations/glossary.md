# Translation Glossary

Terminology reference for translating *Technical Marketing* into JA, ES, FR and DE.

Every entry below is **extracted from pages already live on the site** — it records what
was actually done, not what should be done. Source files: `glossary.html`,
`contributors/mike-moran.html`, `index.html`, `what-is-technical-marketing.html` and
their `ja/`, `es/`, `fr/`, `de/` counterparts.

Domain terms were aligned by their stable `id` anchor in `glossary.html`
(`<div class="term-entry" id="…">`), so EN and locale rows are exact counterparts, not
positional guesses.

---

## 1. The governing convention: English-first

This is the single most important rule, and it is unambiguous in the existing pages.

**ES, FR and DE keep domain and GTM terminology in English.** Of 47 shared glossary
entries, ES localises 4, FR 1, DE 1. Everything else — *Buying committee*, *Champion*,
*Economic buyer*, *Demo coherence*, *Field activation*, *Proof of value*, *Technical
win* — stays in English inside otherwise fully-translated prose.

**JA localises considerably more**, using katakana transliteration for GTM vocabulary
(*Champion* → チャンピオン) and kanji for conceptual terms (*Editorial judgment* →
編集判断). Roughly 24 of 47 entries are localised.

**The publication name is never translated.** "Technical Marketing" appears in English
in all four locales, including inside headings (JA: `Technical Marketingにて`).

When a term is not in the tables below, follow the pattern of its nearest neighbour:
default to English for ES/FR/DE, and to katakana for JA GTM vocabulary.

## 5. Gaps in the existing translations

**The four locale glossaries are stale.** Each has 47 entries; the EN glossary has 63.
These 16 EN terms have **no counterpart in any locale**, so no precedent exists:

`Copley Plan` · `Curse of knowledge` · `Gold demo` · `Industrial engineering
communication` · `Lead churn` · `Mount Stupid` · `Prospect:Vendor Statement Ratio` ·
`Reverse demo` · `Ringi (稟議)` · `Scaling the expert` · `Tacit knowledge transfer` ·
`Talk-to-listen ratio` · `Technical proof demo` · `Virtual twin` · `Visionary
engineering` · `Vision generation demo`

### 5.1 Settled treatments — JA (from `ja/contributors/peter-cohan.html`)

Established by live QA of the first Japanese page to use these terms. **JA only.**
ES, FR and DE are unreviewed and remain deferred.

| Term | JA treatment | Instances on that page |
|---|---|---|
| Copley Plan | **English** | 4 — all consistent |
| reverse demo | **English** | 2 — all consistent |
| talk-to-listen ratio | **English** | 2 — corrected, see below |
| visionary engineering | **English** | 1 |
| technical-proof demo | **English** | 1 |

**The governing rule this settles:** a deferred term stays in English at *every* mention
of the concept in Japanese, including where the English original paraphrases rather than
uses the term.

English can paraphrase a term and stay transparent — a reader meets *"the talk-to-listen
ratio"* and then *"the pitching-to-questioning ratio"* and connects them without effort.
Japanese cannot: `talk-to-listen ratio` followed by 提示と質問の比率 reads as two
unrelated ideas, and the link back to the glossary entry is lost. So the English term is
repeated where English would have varied.

Note the one place this rule does **not** reach: `技術的証明の戦略` (technical-proof
strategy) in the opening paragraph. That renders *"technical-proof strategy"*, an
adjectival phrase, not the glossary term `technical-proof demo`, and it stays translated.
The adjacency of 技術的証明 and `technical-proof demo` on one page is deliberate, not an
oversight.

---

Two of these matter for the pieces now being translated:

- **`Scaling the expert`** is both a glossary term and the title of the article
  published 2026-08-17. A rendering must be decided before it is used in prose.
- **`Ringi (稟議)`** already carries its Japanese original in the EN entry. The JA
  version should presumably use 稟議 alone, but that is a judgment call, not a
  precedent.

These need decisions from you or a native reviewer. **I have not invented renderings
for them** — inventing terminology and presenting it as house style is precisely the
failure mode this glossary exists to prevent.

## 6. Renderings reviewed

### 6.1 JA `A working definition` — CORRECTED

Was `働く定義`. 働く is *to work* in the sense of labouring, so it read closer to *a
definition that has a job*. The intended sense is provisional/operational.

**Changed to `実務上の定義`** in `ja/what-is-technical-marketing.html` (2 occurrences:
the cover `<h1>` and the section `<h2>`).

### 6.2 ES `Técnico de marketing` — NOT an error; do not "fix"

An earlier draft of this glossary claimed `Técnico de marketing` contradicted
`marketing técnico`. **That claim was wrong.** The two forms denote different things
and the ES pages use them correctly and consistently:

| Referent | ES form | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| The **discipline** | `marketing técnico` | 34 occurrences in `es/what-is-technical-marketing.html` |
| The **practitioner** | `técnico de marketing` | glossary definition opens *"Un profesional que gestiona…"*; 35 occurrences across 3 files |

Replacing `técnico de marketing` with `marketing técnico` would collapse the person
into the field and corrupt real sentences — *"El rol del técnico de marketing aquí es
editorial"* would become *"the role of technical marketing here is editorial"*, and the
heading *"El lado del técnico de marketing en la línea"* (EN: *"The technical
marketer's side of the line"*, a possessive of the person) would lose its subject.

> **PARKED — awaiting native-speaker review. Do not sweep.**
>
> A separate, narrower question is open: whether `técnico de marketing` is the best
> Spanish for the practitioner, since it reads literally as *marketing technician*.
> Alternatives: `profesional de marketing técnico`, `especialista en marketing técnico`.
>
> Scope if actioned: 35 occurrences across `es/glossary.html` (22),
> `es/technical-marketing-age-of-ai.html` (12),
> `es/technical-marketing-vs-product-marketing.html` (1).
>
> Deliberately not actioned — a speculative 35-line sweep must not happen mid-
> translation. Requires a native reviewer's decision first.

### 6.3 ES publication-name suffix — one outlier

12 ES pages end their `<title>` with `Technical Marketing`. One does not:
`es/contributors/mike-moran.html` uses `marketing técnico`. FR and DE show no such
outlier (12 each, all `Technical Marketing`).

The rule is that the publication name is never translated, so mike-moran ES was the
defect. **FIXED** — `es/contributors/mike-moran.html` lines 8, 22 and 35 (`<title>`,
`og:title`, `twitter:title`) now read `· Technical Marketing`. ES title suffixes are
now 13/13 consistent. New ES pages must use `· Technical Marketing`.

### 6.5 JSON-LD convention (confirmed 4/4)

- **`@id` is anchored to the EN URL in every locale** — `…/contributors/<name>#person`
  and `#profilepage`. Unanimous across ES, FR, DE, JA. `@id` is a stable entity
  identifier, not an address; do not localise it.
- **`url` is the locale URL.** FR and JA do this correctly; ES and DE do not (§ 6.4).
- `inLanguage` is localised (`es-ES`, `ja-JP`, …), as are ProfilePage `name` and
  `description` and the breadcrumb leaf label.
