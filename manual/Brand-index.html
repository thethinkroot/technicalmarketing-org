Technical Marketing Folio III · Principles
Section I.

Principles.

Ten principles, in this order. When two principles conflict, the higher one governs. These are not for display. They are tests, asked before publication.

  1. Truth before charm.

    The work is judged by whether it is correct, not whether it is clever.

  2. Restraint over decoration.

    Every element earns its place. If a sentence, a rule, a color, or a diagram is not necessary, it is cut.

  3. Citation over assertion.

    Claims made in the publication are sourced. Where a claim cannot be sourced, the claim is qualified or removed.

  4. Specificity over generality.

    Named companies, named roles, named years. “At Red Hat in 2020” rather than “at a major enterprise software company in the early 2020s.”

  5. Independence over alignment.

    The publication is editorially independent. No vendor, employer, or advisor shapes the index or the essays.

  6. Print logic on a web surface.

    Decisions are made as if for a printed quarterly. Web affordances exist only where they serve the reader.

  7. Practitioner-direct, not academic-distant.

    The publication is written for the people doing the work. Academic register is permitted; academic posturing is not.

  8. The work, not the hype.

    The discipline is the subject. Movements, trends, and personalities are subordinate to the work itself.

  9. Trust earned in detail.

    A reader’s confidence is built by accumulated small correctness—accurate dates, real footnotes, correct numerals, defensible claims.

  10. The discipline before the publication.

    Technical Marketing exists to serve the discipline. When the publication’s interests diverge from the discipline's, the discipline wins.

Section II.

Identity and Mission.

The name, the tagline, the mission at three lengths, the trust thesis. What does not appear, by design: AI, the inflection, the founder, ThinkRoot, the year. Missions outlast moments.

The Name.

Technical Marketing.

Always two words, both capitalized, italicized when referenced as a publication title in running prose. The article the is permitted before the name in body prose (“The Technical Marketer, published in Technical Marketing’s first volume…”) but never appears in the masthead.

The URL is technicalmarketing.org. Lowercase in body prose. Small caps in the masthead and OG assets.

The Tagline.

A field manual for the discipline.

Locked. Six words. It appears on the masthead of every page, on the OG image, in the LinkedIn header, in the email footer, and in any context where the publication needs to identify itself in six words.

It is not “A field manual for technical marketing” (the discipline is implicit). It is not “The field manual…” (the indefinite article is correct—the publication does not claim to be the only one). It is not adapted for other registers.

The Mission. Three Lengths.

Six words. Tagline. Masthead use.

A field manual for the discipline.

Twenty-two words. One-sentence positioning.

Technical Marketing is the field manual for a discipline practiced for decades before it had a name. Published quarterly, independently edited.

Fifty-three words. Mission paragraph.

Technical Marketing is the field manual for a discipline practiced for decades before it had a name. The publication exists for the people doing the work—the technical marketers, sales engineers, and product specialists who build the evidence layer that supports a complex sale. Published quarterly. Independently edited.

The Trust Thesis.

Technical Marketing exists because the discipline has no canonical reference. Practitioners operate without a shared body of practice. New entrants arrive with no place to learn what good looks like. Senior people's hard-won judgment is uncollected.

The publication’s job is to become the trust source—the reference that practitioners, hiring managers, analysts, and adjacent disciplines treat as authoritative because it has earned that position through accumulated correctness.

Would the trust source publish this? Would the trust source make this claim? Would the trust source use this word?

Section III.

Editorial Voice.

Field manual register, with explanatory-journalism vocabulary. Institutional-artisanal: the voice of a discipline writing for its own practitioners with the seriousness of a real reference and the directness of a working document.

The Reference Set.

The five lineages the publication draws from. Read them. Their cadence is what the publication is reaching for.

  • WPA Federal Writers' Project, American Guide Series (1935–43). For the practitioner-direct register and the cream-paper convention.
  • Whole Earth Catalog (1968–98). For the catalog logic and the field-manual posture.
  • Edward Tufte’s books. For footnoting density and cross-reference convention.
  • Coudal’s Field Notes. For contemporary American craftsman extension of the lineage.
  • Vox’s explainer journalism. For the definitional-term vocabulary—yellow highlight, footnoted source.

Voice Rules.

Sentences are declarative. No hedges—perhaps, might, arguably, it could be said—unless making an explicit epistemic claim. “The role exists at every major enterprise software company” is correct. “The role can perhaps be said to exist at most enterprise software companies” is wrong.

Short sentences carry the argument. One controlled longer sentence per paragraph provides rhythm. A paragraph of five short declaratives followed by one long architectural sentence is the canonical shape.

Periods on every title, kicker, deck, label, and headline. Wanted. Old Work. Director or VP. Engineering proper. Once committed, commit fully. House rule. Not negotiated per-piece.

Latin numerals for years and folios. Always MMXXVI, never 2026, when the numeral appears in editorial furniture. Arabic numerals for years in body prose.

Real footnotes with real sources. Superscript numeral in body. Citation at foot of folio. No fake citations, no "studies show," no unsourced claims that read as factual.

Diction.

The working lexicon and the banned vocabulary. When in doubt: would a federal field manual from 1942 use this word? If yes, it's safe. If no, it's not.

House Style. The Signature.

When list items are short and parallel, render them with periods, not commas or semicolons. This is the closest thing the publication has to a true house signature. It must be preserved.

  • Product Management
  • Product Marketing
  • Sales Engineering

Punctuation. Numerals. Casing.

Curly quotation marks (“ ”), never straight. Curly apostrophes (’), never straight. Em dashes unspaced (—), never spaced or doubled. Ellipses use the single Unicode character (…), never three periods. Oxford comma always.

Spell out one through nine in body prose. Numerals for ten and above. Numerals always for dates, years, percentages, dollar amounts, measurements, page references.

Sentence case for headlines and decks. Title case for the publication name. Small caps for masthead URL and ISSN furniture—true small caps via font-variant-caps, never CSS uppercase. ALL CAPS reserved for taxonomic labels and the slab masthead.

Visual System.

Five colors. Three faces. One set of marks. No photography, no stock, no clip art, no AI-generated images of people or scenes. The absence is a strength. Preserve it.

Ink.

#1A1A1A

Primary text. Warm near-black. Never pure black. Reads as printer's ink.

#9C2F2F

The single accent. Article numerals. Folio labels. Taxonomic labels. The diamond.

Body Diamond.

Section break. Center of horizontal rules. 6–8px, library red.

Foot of every page. Smaller. Ink color, not red.

Four corners. L-shaped hairlines. Print-plate convention. Hairline gray on web.

Always paired. Never alone. Above and below the masthead. Hairline weight.

Print-Publication Furniture.

ISSN, ANNO, URL—Inter small caps with tracked letterspacing. The convention that the page is a folio of something larger.

Photography and Illustration.

The publication does not use photography. It does not use stock illustration. It does not use clip art. It does not use AI-generated images of people, scenes, or photographic subjects.

It uses original SVG diagrams designed for the publication. Historical screenshots where they document a specific factual claim. Type as visual content—display headlines, marks, ornaments. The absence of photography is a strength.

Technical Marketing Folio VII · Architecture
Section V.

Page Architecture.

How a volume is composed. How an article is composed. How the index is taxonomically organized. The numbering convention that distinguishes furniture from body.

Volume Structure.

Each volume is identified by Roman numeral and season—Vol. I · Spring MMXXVI, Vol. II · Summer MMXXVI, and so on. Volumes are permanent. Each volume lives at its own URL (/vol-01/, /vol-02/) and is preserved after the next volume publishes.

A volume contains a volume cover, an editor’s note, numbered articles (Article I, Article II…), the curated index, and a colophon (typefaces, printing notes, contributors).

Article Anatomy.

Every article has a cover folio (Folio I) with the article number kicker, the article title, the display headline, the secondary headline, and the deck. Numbered folios (Roman) contain the body. Footnotes sit at the foot of each folio where present. Cross-references appear where structurally useful. A closing folio carries the end-mark—the red diamond, centered, below the final line of body.

Section Taxonomy. The Index, Vol. I.

  1. I. Foundations.
  2. II. Career and Hiring.
  3. III. Practice and Method.
  4. IV. Adjacent Disciplines.
  5. V. Critique and Forward Look.

Sections may be added, retitled, or retired between volumes. Section names must read as taxonomic categories, not as marketing copy. “Foundations,” not “The Basics.”

Technical Marketing Folio VIII · Production
Section VI.

Production Standards.

The ten checks that gate publication. The AI-assisted writing protocol. The editorial decisions log. If any check fails, the asset does not ship.

The Pre-Publication Checklist.

Every published asset—folio, cover, OG image, LinkedIn post, web page—passes the following checks before going live.

  1. Voice check.

    Read aloud. Does it sound like Technical Marketing? If not, identify which voice rule it violates and rewrite.

  2. Citation check.

    Every factual claim is sourced or removed. Every footnote points to a real source.

  3. Numeral check.

    Latin numerals in furniture. Arabic in body. Years correctly formatted by context.

  4. Punctuation check.

    Curly quotes, curly apostrophes, em dashes, Oxford commas, periods on titles.

  5. Diction check.

    No banned vocabulary. No hedges. No direct address.

  6. Mark and furniture check.

    Corner registration marks, red diamond, folio number, ANNO, FIRST PRINTING, ISSN line.

  7. Color check.

    Only the five locked colors. No others.

  8. Type check.

    Only the three locked faces, in the assigned roles.

  9. Mobile check.

    Diagrams legible at 375px viewport. Touch targets at 44px or larger.

  10. Date check.

    The publication date is correct. The folio reference is correct.

AI-Assisted Writing Protocol.

Drafting with AI is permitted. Publication of unedited AI output is not.

AI may be used to draft, restructure, or compress copy. The editor must read every sentence in context and rewrite to remove AI tells—superlatives, hedges, false parallelism, the rhetorical question, the “It’s important to note that…” tic, the “In conclusion…” tic. The editor is responsible for every claim. AI may surface candidate facts. Only the editor confirms them against sources.

This manual is the canonical reference for what Technical Marketing sounds like. AI prompts should include or link to this document.

Section VII.

Distribution Conventions.

Where the publication appears. How it identifies itself on each surface. The web is the primary surface. LinkedIn is the primary distribution channel. The register does not change between them.

The Web.

The primary surface. Every folio of every article exists as a permanent URL. Cover folios at /vol-01/article-i/cover. Numbered folios at /vol-01/article-i/folio-viii. The index at /vol-01/.

The web register is identical to the print register. No marketing CTAs. No exit popups. No banner offers. No chat widgets. No animation that does not serve the reader. The page reads as if printed.

LinkedIn.

The primary distribution channel. The publication page is linkedin.com/company/technicalmarketing-org.
