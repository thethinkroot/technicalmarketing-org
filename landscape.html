Technical Marketing Landscape.

How enterprise software, AI, and technology companies staff, title, and place the technical marketing function. Twenty-four companies, verified from public sources. The discipline exists. This is what it looks like.

First compiled June 2026 · Updated July 2026 — added agentic-facing coverage tracking
A note on this update

Agentic AI is beginning to reshape parts of this table, and it will likely become the norm rather than a separate category over time. For now, agentic-facing coverage is tracked in its own dedicated section below rather than folded into the main table, for two reasons: it keeps a still-emerging pattern from being overstated as settled fact, and it preserves a historical record of exactly when and how the function began to shift. See In the Age of AI for the broader argument this table's Agentic-facing section is tracking evidence for. Jump to Agentic-facing coverage ↓.

The technical marketing function has existed under a dozen different titles for six decades. Today it appears in job postings at companies across every category of enterprise software and AI, from cloud infrastructure to developer tooling to security to platform to foundational model companies. The titles are inconsistent. The org placement varies. The work is recognizably similar.

This page documents how the function is currently staffed across a representative sample of companies. It is a working reference, not a comprehensive census. Entries are sourced from public job postings and named practitioners. The page updates each volume as new postings and accounts are verified.

Methodology

Entries are included only when a specific title, org placement, and public source can be verified. Sources are LinkedIn job postings (linked by posting ID), company careers pages, published practitioner accounts, or named-practitioner attribution.

Org placement is categorized as: Marketing (reports through CMO organization), Product (reports through CPO or product leadership), or Presales (reports through VP of Sales Engineering or equivalent field technical leadership). Where the posting describes a hybrid or ambiguous placement, this is noted.

Postings expire. If a linked source is no longer active, the entry remains if the title and placement were verified at time of inclusion, with the source date noted. The discipline's staffing patterns are durable; individual postings are not.

What the table shows

Three org placement archetypes appear consistently. The majority of entries sit inside Marketing and report through a CMO organization, most commonly under a VP or Senior Director of Product Marketing. CrowdStrike names the function explicitly at the VP level as "Product & Technical Marketing." Palo Alto Networks uses a Senior Director anchor. Salesforce (MuleSoft and Slack), Workday, Datadog, LucidLink, JFrog, and Autodesk all place the function inside the marketing org. This is the dominant pattern.

A second group sits at the explicit intersection of product and marketing. Oracle and Databricks describe the role as operating between engineering, product management, and GTM with no single org anchor. AWS sits similarly. Workday names it a "Build & Platform team" role with cross-functional accountability. These entries use language like "intersection of product, engineering, and field" rather than naming a marketing org.

A smaller number sit closer to the field and presales technical organization. Cisco's Director role is the clearest example: a senior IC operating across engineering, product management, sales, and the industry ecosystem. This pattern is more common in networking and infrastructure companies where the field SE org is large and the technical marketing function evolved from it.

The title is not standardized. "Technical Marketing Manager," "Technical Product Marketing Manager," and "Technical Marketing Engineer" all appear across companies doing recognizably similar work. The choice of title reflects company heritage more than functional difference. Companies with engineering-first cultures use "engineer" (Intel, NVIDIA, Cisco, Palo Alto Networks, Pure Storage, Databricks, Elastic). Companies with strong marketing org traditions use "manager" (Salesforce, Workday, ServiceNow, Datadog, LucidLink). The work is the same.

Hardware and semiconductor companies (Intel, NVIDIA) have used these titles formally since the 1960s. The enterprise software version of the role came decades later. The function's roots in hardware are not incidental. They are the origin point. The history is documented in Before the Name.

Agentic-facing coverage

A separate question from staffing overall: has the technical marketing function itself been re-scoped for the agentic AI shift, as distinct from the company shipping agentic AI products? The two are not the same claim. A company can run a large agentic AI engineering effort while its technical marketing function has not yet been re-scoped to match. This table tracks the second claim specifically, sourced independently per entry.

Methodology

Confirmed requires a sourced job posting, named-practitioner account, or on-record interview that explicitly ties a technical marketing or product marketing title to agentic AI scope (agent orchestration, agent frameworks, agentic products by name, or equivalent). Emerging means an AI-scoped technical marketing title is verified but the available source text does not confirm agentic-specific scope. None found means no sourced technical marketing role with agentic scope was located, regardless of the company's broader agentic AI product activity. No data means the search returned no relevant result for this company. No entry in this section is inferred from a company's product announcements alone.

As of July 2026: 9 of 24 tracked entries show sourced, explicit agentic scope in a technical or product marketing role. 2 show an AI-scoped title without confirmed agentic specificity. 12 show no sourced agentic-scoped role despite significant company-wide agentic product investment. 1 returned no data.

What this shows

The technical marketing function and the company's agentic AI product investment are separate claims, and this table keeps them separate on purpose. Several of the companies with the loudest agentic AI product pushes on this table, including CrowdStrike, ServiceNow with Moveworks, Cisco's AgenticOps, IBM's Project Infragraph, and Workday's Illuminate, show no sourced technical marketing role re-scoped to match. Marketing appears to be lagging product at exactly the companies making the largest agentic bets.
