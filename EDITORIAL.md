# Technical Marketing
## Manual of Style and Standards

*Vol. 01 — Anno MMXXVI · First Printing*

---

This document is the canonical reference for *Technical Marketing*. It defines the editorial voice, the house style, the visual system, the page architecture, and the production standards of the publication. Every editorial decision, whether made by a human editor, a contributor, or an AI assistant, should be tested against this manual before publication.

If a question is not answered here, it is answered by the existing body of published work — *Article I: The Technical Marketer*, *Article II: Before the Name*, and the reference *What Is Technical Marketing?* Where those works conflict with this manual, this manual wins and those works are corrected on the next printing.

Maintain this document in the root of the repository as `EDITORIAL.md`. Update it when a canonical decision changes. Mark every change with a date and a reason.

---

## I. Principles

The publication operates on ten principles, in this order. When two principles conflict, the higher one governs.

1. **Truth before charm.** The work is judged by whether it is correct, not whether it is clever.
2. **Restraint over decoration.** Every element earns its place. If a sentence, a rule, a color, or a diagram is not necessary, it is cut.
3. **Citation over assertion.** Claims made in the publication are sourced. Where a claim cannot be sourced, the claim is qualified or removed.
4. **Specificity over generality.** Named companies, named roles, named years. *"At Red Hat in 2020"* rather than *"at a major enterprise software company in the early 2020s."*
5. **Independence over alignment.** The publication is editorially independent. No vendor, employer, or advisor shapes the index or the essays.
6. **Print logic on a web surface.** Decisions are made as if for a printed quarterly. Web affordances exist only where they serve the reader.
7. **Practitioner-direct, not academic-distant.** The publication is written for the people doing the work. Academic register is permitted; academic posturing is not.
8. **The work, not the hype.** The discipline is the subject. Movements, trends, and personalities are subordinate to the work itself.
9. **Trust earned in detail.** A reader's confidence is built by accumulated small correctness — accurate dates, real footnotes, correct numerals, defensible claims.
10. **The discipline before the publication.** *Technical Marketing* exists to serve the discipline. When the publication's interests diverge from the discipline's, the discipline wins.

These principles are not for display. They are tests. *"Does this serve the discipline?"* is a real editorial question, asked before publication.

## II. Identity and Mission

### Name

The publication is *Technical Marketing*. Always two words, both capitalized, italicized when referenced as a publication title in running prose. The article *the* is permitted before the name in body prose (*"The Technical Marketer, published in Technical Marketing's first volume…"*) but never appears in the masthead.

The URL is `technicalmarketing.org`. Render in lowercase in body prose; in small caps in the masthead and OG assets.

### Tagline

> **A field manual for the discipline.**

Locked. This appears on the masthead of every page, on the OG image, in the LinkedIn header, in the email footer, and in any context where the publication needs to identify itself in six words.

It is not modified. It is not "*A field manual for technical marketing*" (the discipline is implicit). It is not "*The field manual…*" (the indefinite article is correct — the publication does not claim to be the only one). It is not adapted for other registers.

### Mission

The mission exists at three lengths, scaffolded so longer versions contain shorter versions verbatim. Use the version that fits the context.

**Six words.** Tagline. Masthead use.

> A field manual for the discipline.

**Twenty-two words.** One-sentence positioning. Use in LinkedIn About, email signatures, contributor bios, partnership conversations.

> *Technical Marketing* is the field manual for a discipline practiced for decades before it had a name. Published quarterly, independently edited.

**Fifty-three words.** Mission paragraph. Use on the About page, in press materials, in correspondence with prospective contributors.

> *Technical Marketing* is the field manual for a discipline practiced for decades before it had a name. The publication exists for the people doing the work — the technical marketers, sales engineers, and product specialists who build the evidence layer that supports a complex sale. Published quarterly. Independently edited.

What is not in any of these versions, by design: AI, the inflection, the founder's name, ThinkRoot, the year, the current moment. Missions outlast moments.

### The Trust Thesis

*Technical Marketing* exists because the discipline has no canonical reference. Practitioners operate without a shared body of practice. New entrants arrive with no place to learn what good looks like. Senior people's hard-won judgment is uncollected. The publication's job is to become the trust source — the reference that practitioners, hiring managers, analysts, and adjacent disciplines treat as authoritative because it has earned that position through accumulated correctness.

Becoming the trust source is the strategic goal. Every editorial decision is measured against it. *Would the trust source publish this? Would the trust source make this claim? Would the trust source use this word?*

## III. Editorial Voice

### Register

The publication's register is **field manual with explanatory-journalism vocabulary.** The reference set:

- *WPA Federal Writers' Project American Guide Series* (1935–43) — for the practitioner-direct register and the cream-paper convention
- *Whole Earth Catalog* (1968–98) — for the catalog logic and the field-manual posture
- *Edward Tufte's books* — for footnoting density and cross-reference convention
- *Coudal's Field Notes* — for contemporary American craftsman extension of the lineage
- *Vox's explainer journalism* — for the definitional-term vocabulary (yellow highlight, footnoted source)

The register is not New Yorker literary. It is not academic. It is not corporate. It is institutional-artisanal: the voice of a discipline writing for its own practitioners with the seriousness of a real reference and the directness of a working document.

### Voice Rules

**Sentences are declarative.** No hedges (*perhaps, might, arguably, it could be said*) unless making an explicit epistemic claim. *"The role exists at every major enterprise software company"* is correct. *"The role can perhaps be said to exist at most enterprise software companies"* is wrong.

**Short sentences carry the argument.** One controlled longer sentence per paragraph provides rhythm. A paragraph of five short declaratives followed by one long architectural sentence is the canonical shape.

**Periods on every title, kicker, deck, label, and headline.** *Wanted. Old Work. Director or VP. Engineering proper.* Once committed, commit fully. This is a house rule. It is not negotiated per-piece.

**Latin numerals for years and folios.** Always *MMXXVI*, never *2026*, when the numeral appears in editorial furniture (masthead, folio, copyright line, ANNO). Arabic numerals for years that appear in body prose (*"in 2009, the discipline…"*).

**Real footnotes with real sources.** Superscript numeral in body. Citation at bottom of folio in the form: *"¹ Author or Publisher, Title, Year."* No fake citations, no "studies show," no unsourced claims that read as factual.

**Cross-references are first-class furniture.** Use the form *see p. IX for the naming →* — italic, with a right-pointing arrow, in tertiary color. Never "click here" or "read more." The reference is a print-publication move and must read as one.

### Diction

**In-register vocabulary** — the publication's working lexicon:

- *the discipline* — when referring to technical marketing
- *practitioners* — collective noun for technical marketers
- *the role* — anaphoric for the position
- *the work* — anaphoric for the practice
- *the evaluation, the demo, the proof of concept, the analyst briefing* — canonical work products, taken with the definite article
- *the judgment* — the discipline's irreducible asset, named with the definite article when invoked as such
- *the field* — permitted where *the discipline* has appeared recently and a substitute is needed
- *the buying committee, the technical evaluation, the displacement deal* — canonical commercial moments

**Out of register vocabulary** — banned outright:

- *leverage, drive, optimize, supercharge, unlock, unleash, harness* — the MBA-marketing lexicon
- *best practices, mindshare, top-of-funnel, synergize, ideate, action* (as verb) — the consultancy vocabulary
- *game-changer, paradigm shift, next-level, world-class, cutting-edge* — the LinkedIn-influencer vocabulary
- *we* without a clear antecedent — collapses editorial distance
- *you* in direct address — reserved for the reader in editorial notes only
- *guys, folks, dudes, you all* — wrong register entirely

When in doubt: would a federal field manual from 1942 use this word? If yes, it's safe. If no, it's not.

### House Style

**Punctuation:**

- Curly quotation marks (" "), never straight (" ")
- Curly apostrophes ('), never straight (')
- Em dashes unspaced (—), never spaced ( — ) or doubled (--)
- Ellipses use the single Unicode character (…), never three periods (...)
- Oxford comma always: *"product, engineering, and marketing"*
- One space after a period, never two
- Possessives are classical: *"Jones's,"* *"Williams's,"* not *"Jones'"* or *"Williams'"*

**Numerals:**

- Spell out one through nine in body prose. *"Three workstreams,"* not *"3 workstreams."*
- Numerals for ten and above: *"twelve pieces indexed across five sections, with thirteen footnotes."* (Note: cardinal numbers above nine that appear in display headlines remain spelled — *"Twelve names. No answer."* — for editorial weight.)
- Numerals always for dates, years, percentages, dollar amounts, measurements, page references
- Years in body prose: *"in 2009"* (Arabic)
- Years in editorial furniture: *"MMXXVI"* (Latin)

**Capitalization:**

- Sentence case for headlines and decks: *"Who the role works with."* Not title case.
- Title case for the publication name: *Technical Marketing.* Not *technical marketing.*
- Small caps for the masthead spelling of the URL and for "FIRST EDITION · ISSN" furniture
- ALL CAPS reserved for taxonomic labels (REPORTS TO, WORKS WITH DAILY) and the slab masthead

**Italics:**

- Titles of published works (books, magazines, publications): *Technical Marketing, The New Yorker*
- Foreign words on first use: *gestalt, sui generis*
- True editorial emphasis — sparing, used at most once per article
- Decks, secondary headlines, captions, footnotes by convention (not for emphasis but as a typographic register)

**Bold:**

- Not used in body prose
- Reserved for the slab masthead, certain display heads, and bold serif labels in structural sections (Article I Folio VIII pattern: *"Director or VP"* in bold serif as the answer to *"REPORTS TO"*)

**Lists:**

- When list items are short and parallel, render them with periods, not commas or semicolons: *"Product Management. Product Marketing. Sales Engineering."* This is the closest thing the publication has to a true house signature and it must be preserved.
- When list items are complex, bullet them and follow standard prose conventions

### Citation and Footnoting

Every claim about a person, a company, a date, a number, a publication, a regulation, or a market condition must be footnotable. If the source is in dispute, the claim is rephrased to remove the dispute or removed entirely.

Footnote format:

> ¹ Author or organization, *Title or publication*, date.

Example, as used in Article I, Folio VIII:

> ¹ CrowdStrike Senior Technical Marketing Manager JD, Built In, November 2025.

Cross-references between folios use the form:

> *see p. IX for the naming →*

These are not optional editorial flourishes. They are the publication's evidence layer made visible.

### Cross-References to Adjacent Publications

When citing work outside *Technical Marketing*, identify the author and the publication. *"Bryan Friedman, on his blog, in November 2025."* Not *"a recent blog post on the topic."*

When citing external work that has earned status — Gong's analysis, the Product Marketing Alliance, Peter Cohan's *Great Demo!* — give the work the respect of a full attribution. The publication's credibility rises with the credibility of what it cites.

## IV. Visual System

### Palette

Five colors, locked. No other colors appear in canonical assets.

**Paper** — the background of every page and asset.
- Proposed hex: `#F4EFE0` (warm wheat, uncoated-stock simulation)
- *Verify against existing assets and lock the exact hex from the Photoshop or design tool source.*

**Ink** — the primary text color.
- Proposed hex: `#1A1A1A` (warm near-black, never pure `#000`)
- Reads as printer's ink rather than digital black

**Library red** — the single accent.
- Proposed hex: `#9C2F2F` (muted oxblood)
- *Verify against existing assets.*
- Used for: article numerals (ARTICLE I.), folio labels (FOLIO I), taxonomic labels (REPORTS TO), the role-name treatment in red caps, the red diamond ornament, registration mark corners

**Butter yellow** — the highlight, used at low opacity behind display words.
- Proposed hex: `#F0D560` at approximately 50–60% opacity
- *Verify against existing assets.*
- Used only behind selected display headlines (*Wanted., Old Work.*) to mark the definitional term in the Vox-explainer tradition

**Hairline gray** — for rules and dividers.
- Proposed hex: `#A8A296` (warm gray, derived from the paper)
- Used for horizontal rules, registration marks where not red, footnote separators

If a sixth color is needed for a future asset, the proposal must come through editorial review. Add it to this manual or reject it.

### Typography

The system is three faces. Confirm and lock the exact files before further production.

**Display slab serif** — the masthead, the section headlines, the taxonomic labels in caps.
- Proposed face: **Roboto Slab Bold** (Google Fonts, free, widely licensed)
- *Verify against the existing asset's source file. If different, note here and use that.*
- Used for: TECHNICAL MARKETING masthead, primary article display heads (*Old Work., Wanted.*), the red caps taxonomic labels

**Editorial serif** — Fraunces, full family, optical sizing engaged.
- Roman 400, 500 for body and headings
- Italic 400 for decks, captions, footnotes, body when used as editorial register
- SemiBold or Bold 600 for primary information lines (*Director or VP.* as the answer to *REPORTS TO*)
- Optical size axis used: text (9–14pt) for body, display (24pt+) for headings

**Body sans** — Inter.
- 500 weight for the masthead furniture (FIRST EDITION · ISSN, ANNO MMXXVI), the URL, the small footer text
- Used only where slab and serif would be wrong — small caps furniture, navigation labels on web

**Typographic hierarchy** — fixed roles by face:

| Element | Face | Weight | Treatment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Masthead wordmark | Slab serif | Bold | ALL CAPS |
| Article number kicker | Slab serif | Bold | ALL CAPS, red |
| Article title | Fraunces | Italic 400 | Sentence case |
| Display headline | Slab serif | Bold | Sentence case |
| Secondary headline | Fraunces | Italic 400 | Sentence case |
| Deck | Fraunces | Italic 400 | Sentence case, smaller |
| Body roman | Fraunces | 400 | Sentence case |
| Body italic | Fraunces | Italic 400 | Used as editorial register |
| Bold serif label (answer) | Fraunces | SemiBold or Bold | Sentence case |
| Taxonomic label | Slab serif | Bold | ALL CAPS, red |
| Footnote | Fraunces | Italic 400 | Small, indented |
| Folio | Slab serif | Bold | ALL CAPS, red |
| Cross-reference | Fraunces | Italic 400 | Small, with arrow |
| Furniture text (ISSN, ANNO) | Inter | 500 | Small caps |

**Micro-typography rules:**

- Hanging punctuation enabled where the renderer permits
- `text-wrap: pretty` enabled on all body prose (modern CSS)
- OpenType oldstyle numerals (`font-variant-numeric: oldstyle-nums`) for body prose
- OpenType lining numerals for taxonomic labels and folios
- True small caps (`font-variant-caps: small-caps`) for masthead furniture — never CSS `text-transform: uppercase`
- Hyphenation enabled for body prose (`hyphens: auto`) — disabled for headlines

### Marks and Furniture

**The red diamond.** The publication's signature mark. Two sizes:

- **Body diamond** — small, red (`#9C2F2F`), used as a section break in the center of horizontal rules. Rendered as a 6–8px rotated square with no border.
- **Folio diamond** — smaller still, dark ink color, centered between FOLIO and the printing line at the foot of every page

**Corner registration marks.** L-shaped hairlines in the four corners of each page or LinkedIn asset. Each leg approximately 24px long, hairline weight (`stroke-width: 0.5`). The marks signal print-plate registration and are part of the publication's institutional voice. They appear on every cover, every folio, every OG image. Render in hairline gray on web, in ink black on covers.

**Horizontal rules.** Hairline weight (`stroke-width: 0.5`), warm gray. Used above and below the masthead, between sections, above the folio. Always paired — a rule appears with another rule, never alone, in the masthead-stack convention.

**The yellow highlight swipe.** A rectangular fill of butter yellow at 50–60% opacity positioned behind a single display word. The swipe extends slightly beyond the cap-height of the word and stops short of the descenders. Used on selected display headlines only — *Wanted., Old Work.* The highlight marks the definitional term. Applied judiciously: not every display word earns one. Editor's call, made per piece.

**Print-publication furniture** — appears on every cover and every folio:

- *FIRST EDITION · ISSN 0000-0001* (top left) — *if an ISSN is later obtained from the Library of Congress, the placeholder is replaced.*
- *ANNO MMXXVI* / *ISSUE I · N°1* (top right)
- *TECHNICALMARKETING.ORG* (right side, small caps)
- *A field manual for the discipline.* (italic subtitle, below masthead)
- *FOLIO [Roman numeral]* (foot, left)
- *FIRST PRINTING · MMXXVI* (foot, right)
- *Red diamond* (foot, center)
- Corner registration marks (four corners)

### Photography and Illustration

The publication does not use photography. It does not use stock illustration. It does not use clip art. It does not use AI-generated images of people, scenes, or photographic subjects.

It uses:

- Original SVG diagrams designed for the publication (the function matrix, the calibration band, the evaluation lifecycle, the AI exposure quadrant)
- Historical screenshots and archival images where they document a specific factual claim (the 2009 Wayback Machine capture is the canonical example)
- Type as visual content — display headlines, marks, ornaments

The absence of photography is a strength. Preserve it.

## V. Page Architecture

### Volume Structure

Each volume is identified by Roman numeral and season: *Vol. I · Spring MMXXVI, Vol. II · Summer MMXXVI*, and so on. Volumes are permanent. Each volume's content lives at its own URL (`/vol-01/`, `/vol-02/`) and is preserved after the next volume publishes.

A volume contains:

- A volume cover (`/vol-01/cover`)
- An editor's note
- Numbered articles (Article I, Article II, …)
- The curated index
- A colophon (typefaces, printing notes, contributors)

### Article Anatomy

Every article has:

- A cover folio (`Folio I`) with the article number kicker, the article title, the display headline, the secondary headline, and the deck
- Numbered folios (Roman) containing the body of the article
- Footnotes at the foot of each folio, where present
- Cross-references where structurally useful (*see p. IX for the naming →*)
- A closing folio with an end-mark — the red diamond, centered, below the final line of body

### Section Taxonomy (in the index)

The index is organized into Roman-numbered sections. Current sections (Vol. I):

- I. Foundations
- II. Career and Hiring
- III. Practice and Method
- IV. Adjacent Disciplines
- V. Critique and Forward Look

Sections may be added, retitled, or retired between volumes. Section names must read as taxonomic categories, not as marketing copy. *"Foundations,"* not *"The Basics."*

### Numbering Conventions

- Volumes: Roman numerals (I, II, III, IV…)
- Articles within a volume: Roman numerals (I, II, III…)
- Folios within an article: Roman numerals (I, II, III…)
- Sections within the index: Roman numerals
- Footnotes: Arabic numerals, superscript, restarting per folio
- Index entries: Arabic numerals if numbered at all (typically unnumbered, organized by section)
- Years in furniture: Latin (MMXXVI)
- Years in body prose: Arabic (2026)

## VI. Production Standards

### Before Publication

Every published asset — folio, cover, OG image, LinkedIn post, web page — must pass the following checks before going live.

1. **Voice check.** Read aloud. Does it sound like *Technical Marketing*? If not, identify which voice rule it violates and rewrite.
2. **Citation check.** Every factual claim is sourced or removed. Every footnote points to a real source.
3. **Numeral check.** Latin numerals in furniture; Arabic in body. Years correctly formatted by context.
4. **Punctuation check.** Curly quotes, curly apostrophes, em dashes, Oxford commas, periods on titles.
5. **Diction check.** No banned vocabulary. No hedges. No direct address.
6. **Mark and furniture check.** Corner registration marks, red diamond, folio number, ANNO, FIRST PRINTING, ISSN line.
7. **Color check.** Only the five locked colors. No others.
8. **Type check.** Only the three locked faces, in the assigned roles.
9. **Mobile check** (web only). Diagrams legible at 375px viewport. Touch targets at 44px or larger.
10. **Date check.** The publication date is correct. The folio reference is correct.
11. **URL and canonical check** (web only). Run `node tools/seo-check.mjs` from the repository root. Every sitemap entry, internal link, language-picker link, footer link and hreflang tag must point at the canonical URL — the exact scheme and trailing-slash form declared in that page's `<link rel="canonical">`. A link that redirects is a failure, not a convenience: Google Search Console reports it as *Page with redirect* and fails sitemap validation.
12. **Term-consistency check** (web only). Run `node tools/term-check.mjs` from the repository root. Every settled glossary term (`translations/glossary.md` § 5) must render the same way at every mention within a locale page, a rejected rendering must never reappear, and a settled-translated term must never be left in English.

If any check fails, the asset does not ship.

The URL and canonical check and the term-consistency check both also run automatically as a `pre-push` git hook. Enable it once per clone:

```
git config core.hooksPath .githooks
```

Add `--live` to `seo-check.mjs` to verify against the deployed site over the network, and `--show-known` to list the pre-existing issues recorded in `tools/seo-check-ignore.txt`. Entries in that file are real defects that are not yet fixed; delete a line the moment its issue is resolved, because a stale entry silently hides a regression.

### Glossary auto-linking

Once seo-check and term-check both pass for a page — never before, since linking bakes in whatever rendering happened to be current — run `node tools/glossary-link.mjs` from the repository root to wire the first plain-text mention of every settled glossary term, per article, per locale, into a link to that locale's own glossary anchor. It is an editorial tool, not a gate: unlike the two checks above, it writes to the working tree, so it is run deliberately and is not part of the pre-push hook. Use `--check` for a dry run that reports what would change without writing, and `--verbose` to also see every term the tool considered and skipped. Re-running is a no-op once every article is wired — a term already linked anywhere in a file is left alone.

### AI-Assisted Writing Protocol

Drafting with AI is permitted. Publication of unedited AI output is not.

- AI may be used to draft, restructure, or compress copy
- The editor must read every sentence in context and rewrite to remove AI tells: superlatives, hedges, false parallelism, the rhetorical question, the *"It's important to note that…"* tic, the *"In conclusion…"* tic
- The editor is responsible for every claim. AI may surface candidate facts. Only the editor confirms them against sources.
- This manual is the canonical reference for what *Technical Marketing* sounds like. AI prompts should include or link to this document.

### Editorial Decisions Log

When a canonical decision changes — a color is relocked, a tagline is rephrased, a banned word becomes permitted — the change is logged in this manual with date and rationale. The log is the publication's institutional memory.

Initial log:

## VII. Distribution Conventions

### Web

The web is the primary surface. Every folio of every article exists as a permanent URL. Cover folios live at `/vol-01/article-i/cover`; numbered folios at `/vol-01/article-i/folio-viii`; the index at `/vol-01/`. URLs use Arabic numerals (`folio-8`, not `folio-viii`) for compatibility but the displayed numeral is Roman.

The web register is identical to the print register. The site does not make web concessions — no marketing CTAs, no exit popups, no banner offers, no chat widgets, no animation that does not serve the reader. The page reads as if printed.

### LinkedIn

LinkedIn is the primary distribution channel. The publication's LinkedIn page is `linkedin.com/company/technicalmarketing-org`.

LinkedIn posts:

- Take the form of a folio image (1080 × 1350, the LinkedIn portrait specification) in the field-manual register
- Include the post copy as a single short paragraph in the publication's voice
- Link to the canonical web URL for the folio
- Use the publication's voice rules in the post body, not LinkedIn's default register

LinkedIn cover image: a designed asset in the masthead register, 1128 × 191 specification.

LinkedIn About: the twenty-two-word mission statement.

### OG and Share Assets

Every URL has an Open Graph image. Default OG image lives at the root (`/og-image.png`). Per-article and per-volume OGs live at predictable paths. All OG assets are in the field-manual register — wheat paper, slab masthead, red diamond, corner registration marks.

OG specifications: 1200 × 630 PNG, RGB, no transparency. File size under 100KB where possible.

### Email

Future newsletter, if and when published, uses the same voice and the same visual register. Field manual on paper, field manual on the web, field manual in the inbox.

### Cross-Reference to Adjacent Properties

*Technical Marketing* is editorially independent. The publication does not promote, link to as a CTA, or otherwise serve as a funnel for any other property — including the editor's own. The editor's name appears in the byline, with a link to a personal site where appropriate. Beyond that, the publication is self-contained.
